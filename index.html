About Me

My background is in algorithms and graph theory, which I studied at the University of Waterloo.

My first research project applied graph neural networks to the Steiner tree problem. I proved conditions under which a classical approximation algorithm is guaranteed to make choices consistent with an optimal solution, and used a learned model only where that guarantee fails. The proof was the part of the project I found most rewarding. Most of the work, however, was training and benchmarking models, and it showed me that I want to work on problems where the main results are theorems rather than experiments.

This led me to randomized numerical linear algebra (RandNLA). RandNLA uses random sampling and random projections to solve large matrix problems, such as least squares and low-rank approximation, and proves that the answers are accurate with high probability. It brings together the areas I enjoy most: linear algebra, probability, and the analysis of algorithms. I am currently studying sketching, subspace embeddings, and randomized low-rank approximation, and I plan to pursue graduate study in this area.
